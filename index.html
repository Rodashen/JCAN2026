About EPS Topik

The EPS or Employment Permit System is a program by the South Korean government that allows foreign workers from partner countries to work legally in Korea in industries such as Manufacturing, Construction, Agriculture, Fisheries, Service Industry, and Shipbuilding, subject to the sectors open under the current recruitment notice.

To work in Korea, you need to pass the EPS Topik exam, which tests your Korean language skills and understanding of Korean culture. The exam is conducted by the Human Resources Development Service of Korea (HRD Korea).

To prepare for the EPS Topik exam, you can take classes at JCAN Korean Language Center, where we offer comprehensive training programs that cover all aspects of the exam.

Why work in Korea?

Working in South Korea offers opportunities to earn, develop workplace skills, and experience a different culture.

Experience autumn, winter, and spring while improving your Korean language skills and gaining valuable work experience.

Benefits of Working in South Korea

Basic salary may reach ₱90,000 or more per month, depending on the employment contract and exchange rate. Actual take-home pay varies.

Government-to-government application
  • DMW (formerly POEA) is the Philippine government sending agency for EPS. Apply only through the official process.
  • No placement fee. Lawful deductions, including taxes and applicable insurance contributions, may still apply.
  • Korea’s minimum wage is reviewed annually; individual salary increases depend on the legal minimum and contract.
Leave and additional pay
  • Paid annual leave (yeoncha): generally 15 days after one year with at least 80% attendance. During the first year, one day is earned per fully attended month, where the law applies.
  • Overtime: generally at least 150% of ordinary pay in total. Holiday work attracts an additional premium of at least 50% for up to 8 hours and 100% beyond 8 hours, where applicable.
  • Night work (10 PM–6 AM): an additional premium of at least 50%, where applicable.
  • Maternity leave: generally 90 days, or 120 days for multiple births; spouse childbirth leave: 20 days. Eligibility and payment rules apply.
Four major social insurance schemes
  • National Health Insurance — support for covered medical treatment and check-ups.
  • National Pension Scheme (NPS) — retirement benefits, subject to coverage and eligibility.
  • Employment Insurance — eligible unemployment, maternity, and childcare benefits; coverage varies by visa and enrollment.
  • Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance — eligible treatment costs and lost-income benefits for work-related injury or illness.
Worker protection and support
  • Income tax refunds may be available after tax settlement, depending on tax paid and applicable deductions.
  • Severance pay after at least one year of continuous service, subject to working-hour and other legal requirements.
  • Departure Guarantee Insurance — helps secure eligible severance benefits upon permanent departure.
  • Wage Guarantee Insurance — covers eligible unpaid wages within policy limits when an employer defaults.
  • Health check-ups according to employment and insurance requirements.
Employment and future opportunities
  • Initial employment period of up to 3 years, with an approved extension of up to 1 year and 10 months.
  • Eligible sincere workers may qualify for re-entry employment. This is not an automatic 10-year contract.
  • Opportunities to apply for a skilled-worker visa, subject to immigration criteria and approval.
  • Food, accommodation, and transportation may be provided free or at a cost, depending on the employer and signed contract.

Benefits depend on the workplace, contract, insurance coverage, and current rules. Official references: DMW EPS, Korean Labor Standards Act, EPS insurance, and Minimum Wage Council.

The process

To become an EPS worker, first, you need to register and take the EPS-TOPIK Korean language test. Once you pass the exam, you can apply for job placement through the official EPS process.

After securing a job offer, you will need to complete the necessary paperwork and obtain a work visa. The EPS program also provides support for workers in Korea, including language training and cultural orientation.

At JCAN Korean Language Center, we offer comprehensive training programs that cover all aspects of the EPS process, from language preparation to assistance with application requirements. Job matching and deployment are handled through the official government process. Our experienced instructor will guide you through every step, ensuring that you are well-prepared for your journey to Korea.

Qualifications
  • Age: 18 to 38 years old at registration, subject to the announced birthdate range
  • Male or female
  • Physically and mentally fit for the required work
  • No disqualifying criminal conviction
  • No record of deportation from South Korea
  • Must have a valid passport
  • No restrictions on leaving the Philippines or entering South Korea
  • No color blindness or color weakness
  • Must meet the required medical screening standards
  • Must not use illegal drugs

This is an overview. Additional conditions, including previous E-9 / E-10 stays, may apply. Check the latest official DMW qualifications and recruitment announcement.
